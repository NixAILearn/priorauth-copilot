# Riverbend Orthopedics: Office Visit

**Patient:** Kestrel Underhill · **DOB:** 10/30/1958 · **Date of visit: 09/03/2026**
**Provider:** Dr. Farrukh Strathmore, Orthopedic surgeon · **Referring:** Dr. Hollis Valcourt

## Chief complaint

Right knee pain with mechanical symptoms.

## HPI

Mr. Underhill is a 67 y/o M with right knee pain for about two years, gradually worse. Pain is medial, aching, worse on stairs and after walking. Over the past few months the right knee catches suddenly mid-stride, with a brief block before it moves again, several times a week, most recently this week. 

Treatment so far: naproxen and a supervised strengthening program starting 05/18/2026; 13 therapy visits through 08/24/2026, with modest gains but persistent pain. Naproxen stopped when therapy ended.

Function: walks about 15 to 20 minutes before needing to rest, uses the railing on stairs, and has stopped longer outings with family because of the knee. Sleep is sometimes disturbed by aching after a busy day.

## Review of systems

Constitutional: no fever, chills or weight loss. CV: no chest pain. Resp: no shortness of breath. GI: no bleeding. Skin: no rash or wounds over the knee. Neuro: no numbness or weakness.  All other systems reviewed and negative.

## Past medical history

- Essential hypertension

## Past surgical history

- No prior knee surgery on either side
- Appendectomy, childhood

## Medications

Reconciled today; see the medication list.

## Physical examination

**Vitals:** BP 134/80, HR 68.

General: well appearing, in no distress.
Gait: mildly antalgic on the right.
Right knee: small effusion, medial joint line tenderness, patellofemoral crepitus. Skin intact, no warmth or redness. Range of motion 0 to 120 degrees. Ligaments stable to varus, valgus, Lachman and drawer testing.
Opposite knee: no effusion, mild crepitus.
Hips: painless motion.
Neurovascular: sensation intact; pedal pulses 2+.

## Imaging

Standing radiographs of the right knee reviewed: moderate medial narrowing, no loose body. MRI ordered; result pending at the time of this visit.

## Assessment

Right knee osteoarthritis with mechanical catching after more than 12 weeks of conservative care. MRI to define the cause of the catching.

## Plan

1. Discussed arthroscopic debridement of the right knee to address the mechanical symptoms and the unstable lesion, with realistic expectations: the procedure treats the mechanical problem, and arthritis pain may persist.
2. Risks reviewed: infection, bleeding, blood clots, stiffness, persistent pain and the need for further surgery.
3. Request prior authorization from Northwind Health Plan.
4. Continue acetaminophen as needed and home exercises until surgery.

## Discussion

We reviewed the imaging together using a knee model. The patient asked about recovery: most people walk the same day with crutches for a few days, return to light activity within two to three weeks, and continue strengthening exercises afterward. Questions were answered and the patient wishes to proceed.
