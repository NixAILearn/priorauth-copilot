**Patient:** Nadir Holloway-Tan · **DOB:** 02/07/1953 · **Date of visit: 09/15/2026**
**Provider:** Dr. Quilla Iverholt, Orthopedic surgeon · **Referring:** Dr. Zeno Ostrander

## Chief complaint

Right knee pain with mechanical symptoms.

## HPI

Mr. Holloway-Tan is a 73 y/o M with right knee pain for about two years, gradually worse. Pain is medial, aching, worse on stairs and after walking. Over the past few months the right knee catches suddenly mid-stride, with a brief block before it moves again, several times a week, most recently this week. 

Treatment so far: naproxen and supervised physical therapy from 05/30/2026 through 09/05/2026, modest gains, persistent pain. Naproxen stopped when therapy ended.

Function: walks about 15 to 20 minutes before needing to rest, uses the railing on stairs, and has stopped longer outings with family because of the knee. Sleep is sometimes disturbed by aching after a busy day.

## Imaging

Standing radiographs of the right knee from 09/01/2026: moderate medial joint space narrowing with osteophytes, no loose body. The radiologist reports Kellgren-Lawrence grade 2. MRI obtained by the primary care office on 03/28/2026: full-thickness medial femoral condyle cartilage defect with an unstable flap.

## Assessment

Right knee osteoarthritis with mechanical catching and an unstable cartilage lesion on MRI, after more than 12 weeks of conservative care.

## Plan

1. Discussed arthroscopic debridement of the right knee to address the mechanical symptoms and the unstable lesion, with realistic expectations: the procedure treats the mechanical problem, and arthritis pain may persist.
2. Risks reviewed: infection, bleeding, blood clots, stiffness, persistent pain and the need for further surgery.
3. Request prior authorization from Northwind Health Plan.
4. Continue acetaminophen as needed and home exercises until surgery.

## Discussion

We reviewed the imaging together using a knee model. The patient asked about recovery: most people walk the same day with crutches for a few days, return to light activity within two to three weeks, and continue strengthening exercises afterward. Questions were answered and the patient wishes to proceed.
