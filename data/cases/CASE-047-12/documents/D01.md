# Riverbend Orthopedics: New Patient Visit

**Patient:** Talia Yardley · **DOB:** 05/03/1961 · **Date of visit: 10/14/2026**
**Provider:** Dr. Jory Lindqvist-Amari, Orthopedic surgeon · **Referring:** Dr. Elowen Kittredge
**MA:** Lucan Jessop

## Chief complaint

Right knee pain for two years.

## HPI

Ms. Yardley is a 65 y/o F referred for evaluation of chronic right knee pain. Pain began gradually about two years ago without injury and has slowly worsened. It is medial and anterior, aching, rated 6/10 at worst, and worse with stairs, getting up from a chair and walking more than 20 minutes. Her symptoms are pain, morning stiffness and crepitus; the morning stiffness lasts under 30 minutes. She denies locking or catching. No instability or giving way. No night pain that wakes her. No numbness or tingling.

Conservative treatment: she started naproxen 500 mg twice daily and a supervised PT program in May. She completed 12 PT visits through 08/19/2026 with modest strength gains but persistent pain, and stopped naproxen in August because of stomach upset. She uses acetaminophen as needed with partial relief. She would like a procedure that can relieve the pain.

## Review of systems

Constitutional: no fever, chills or weight loss. CV: no chest pain. Resp: no shortness of breath. GI: stomach upset resolved after stopping naproxen. MSK: as per HPI; mild L knee aching after long walks. Skin: no rashes. Neuro: no weakness. Psych: no depression. All other systems reviewed and negative.

## Imaging

The radiologist reports Kellgren-Lawrence grade 2. No MRI has been obtained.

## Assessment

65 y/o F with symptomatic primary osteoarthritis of the right knee, medial predominant, with two years of pain despite 12 weeks of supervised PT and an NSAID trial. No mechanical symptoms and a knee that reaches full extension on exam.

## Plan

1. Discussed options at length: continued home exercise, topical NSAID, weight management, and arthroscopic debridement of the right knee. Explained that debridement mainly addresses mechanical problems and that relief of arthritis pain is often limited, and that knee replacement may be a future option.
2. The patient prefers to pursue arthroscopic debridement of the right knee given her persistent pain. We will request prior authorization from Northwind Health Plan.
3. Continue acetaminophen as needed and home exercise program.
4. Return after the authorization decision, sooner if symptoms change.
