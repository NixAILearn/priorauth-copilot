## Data reviewed

## Counseling

We discussed how upper airway stimulation works: a small generator placed under the collarbone senses breathing and stimulates the nerve that moves the tongue forward during sleep. The patient turns the device on at bedtime with a handheld remote and off in the morning. We reviewed that it is not a cure, that most patients still need follow-up sleep studies to adjust settings, and that weight changes can affect results. Alternatives discussed included continued positive airway pressure with new interfaces, an oral appliance, positional therapy and palate or tongue-base surgery. The patient chose to pursue the implant. Written material was provided.

## Assessment

68-year-old man with moderate-to-severe OSA, failed CPAP, BMI 29.8 kg/m2, and complete concentric collapse at the soft palate on DISE. Explained that this pattern predicts a poor response. Patient asks that authorization be requested.

## Plan

1. Reviewed the endoscopy findings and the alternatives, including palate surgery.
2. At the patient's request, submit prior authorization for the implant to Northwind Health Plan.
3. Return to discuss palate surgery if the implant is not approved.
