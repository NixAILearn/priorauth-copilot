# NHP-MP-047: Arthroscopic debridement for osteoarthritis of the knee

**Northwind Health Plan · Medical Policy · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-01-01**

> Fictional policy for a non-commercial demo. Northwind Health Plan is not a real health plan. This document is not medical advice and does not describe any real payer's coverage.

## 1. Policy statement

Arthroscopic debridement removes loose fragments, unstable torn tissue or damaged cartilage from inside the knee through small incisions. For a knee with osteoarthritis, Northwind does not consider this surgery medically necessary as a treatment for the arthritis itself, including knee pain, stiffness or swelling. Northwind covers it only in one narrow situation: the member has symptoms other than pain alone, namely a **mechanical symptom** (section 7.3) caused by a **mechanical lesion** (section 7.4) seen on imaging, the arthritis is not **severe** (section 7.5), and the criteria in sections 3 and 4 are met with nothing in section 5 applying. Requests that do not clearly meet these criteria are reviewed by a Northwind clinician.

## 3. Coverage criteria

All of the following must be met unless a criterion says otherwise.

**3.1 Side named.** The request names the **requested knee** as the right knee or the left knee.

**3.2 Side consistent.** No document included with the request places the diagnosis, the **mechanical symptom** or the imaging findings used to meet sections 3 and 4 in the knee opposite the **requested knee**.

**3.3 Mechanical symptom present.** At least one **mechanical symptom** of the **requested knee** is **documented** in a clinician note dated within 3 months before the request date.

**3.4 Weight-bearing radiographs.** A report of **weight-bearing radiographs** of the **requested knee**, taken within 12 months before the request date, is included with the request.

**3.5 Arthritis not severe.** The most recent **weight-bearing radiograph** report that meets 3.4 grades the **requested knee** at no more than Kellgren–Lawrence grade 3, using the **highest compartment grade** (section 7.6).

## 4. Alternative pathways

All numbered items in this section are required. Each can be met in more than one way.

**4.1 Conservative care or locked knee.** Any one of the following:

- (a) completion of **adequate conservative care** (section 7.7), with a **mechanical symptom** still **documented** after it ended
- (b) a **locked knee** (section 7.8), **documented** within 1 month before the request date

**4.2 Lesion on imaging.** Any one of the following:

- (a) an MRI report of the **requested knee**, with the scan done within 6 months before the request date, that describes a **mechanical lesion**
- (b) a **weight-bearing radiograph** report of the **requested knee** that meets 3.4 and describes a **loose body** (section 7.4(a))

## 5. Contraindications and exclusions

### 5.1 Contraindications

Coverage criteria are not met if any of the following is documented.

- **5.1.1** The **requested knee** has a total or partial knee replacement.
- **5.1.2** Active infection in the **requested knee**, or in the skin over it.
- **5.1.3** The treating surgeon or an anesthesia clinician states that elective knee surgery is currently unsafe for the member.

### 5.2 Exclusions

Northwind does not cover the service under this policy in the following situations.

- **5.2.1** The planned procedure is washing out the joint (lavage) without removing any tissue.
- **5.2.2** An arthroscopic procedure was performed on the **requested knee** within 12 months before the request date.
- **5.2.3** The procedure is planned in the same operation as a knee replacement of the **requested knee**.

## 7. Definitions

**7.1 Request date.** The date Northwind receives a complete prior authorization request. All time windows in this policy count back from this date unless a criterion names another date.

**7.2 Documented.** Stated in a dated clinical record signed by a licensed clinician and included with the request. A statement made only on the request form is not documentation. An imaging report signed by the reading radiologist is a clinical record.

**7.3 Mechanical symptom.** A symptom showing that something inside the knee is physically blocking movement, as opposed to pain limiting movement. Only these two count:

- (a) **Locking:** the knee becomes stuck so that it cannot be straightened, and stays stuck until the member moves it in a particular way or it suddenly releases.
- (b) **Catching:** a sudden, brief block to bending or straightening the knee that the member feels as the joint catching on something, recurring with movement.

None of the following is a mechanical symptom, alone or together: pain; stiffness, including morning stiffness or stiffness after rest; swelling; grinding or crepitus (a crunching feel or sound); clicking or popping without a block to movement; giving way or buckling without a block to movement; and "pseudo-locking", where the member avoids moving the knee because of pain. A note that uses the word "locking" or "catching" counts only if it, or another note, describes what the member experiences in terms that fit (a) or (b).

**7.4 Mechanical lesion.** A structure inside the knee that imaging shows can physically block movement. Only these count:

- (a) **Loose body:** a free fragment of bone or cartilage inside the joint.
- (b) **Displaced or unstable meniscal tear:** a meniscal tear in which a fragment has moved out of its normal position, or which the radiologist describes as a flap, bucket-handle or displaced tear.
- (c) **Unstable cartilage flap:** a flap of joint cartilage that the radiologist describes as partly detached or unstable.

A degenerative meniscal tear, meniscal fraying or signal change, cartilage thinning, bone marrow edema, bone spurs (osteophytes) and joint fluid are not mechanical lesions. A finding the report describes only as possible, probable, suspected or not excluded does not describe a mechanical lesion.

**7.5 Severe.** Kellgren–Lawrence grade 4 in any compartment of the **requested knee**, as defined in 7.6.

**7.6 Kellgren–Lawrence grade; highest compartment grade.** The Kellgren–Lawrence (KL) grade is a 0-to-4 scale of osteoarthritis on radiographs, where 4 is the most severe. The **highest compartment grade** is the highest grade given to any compartment of the knee (medial, lateral or patellofemoral). If the report gives one overall grade, that grade is used. If the report gives no grade but states that any compartment has complete loss of joint space or bone-on-bone contact, the knee is treated as grade 4. If the report gives no grade and makes neither statement, criterion 3.5 is not shown.

**7.7 Adequate conservative care.** Non-surgical treatment of the **requested knee** that meets all of the following:

- (a) structured exercise therapy: supervised physical therapy, or a home exercise program prescribed by a clinician and **documented** as followed at a later visit
- (b) at least one of: an oral or topical anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID); acetaminophen, when an NSAID is **documented** as unsafe for the member; or an injection of corticosteroid into the **requested knee**
- (c) items (a) and (b) together spanned at least 12 weeks, from the start of the first measure to the end of the last
- (d) the care ended, or was still ongoing, within 12 months before the request date

**7.8 Locked knee.** On a clinician's examination, the **requested knee** cannot be straightened fully, actively or passively, because of a block inside the joint, and the note states that the block is new for this member. A long-standing loss of straightening from arthritis (a flexion contracture) is not a locked knee.

**7.9 Requested knee.** The knee named on the request as the site of NHP-P-3005.

**7.10 Time windows.** "Within N months before the request date" includes the boundary day and is counted in calendar months: the window starts on the same calendar day N months before the request date, or on the last day of that month if it has no such day, and ends on the request date. Durations in weeks (such as 7.7(c)) are counted in days, 7 per week, and are not time windows.

**7.11 Weight-bearing radiograph.** A plain radiograph of the knee taken while the member stands on it. A report must say the views were standing or weight-bearing; a radiograph taken lying down does not meet 3.4 or 3.5.

## 8. Clinical basis

Several randomized trials, including trials with a sham-surgery control, found that arthroscopic debridement or washing out of an osteoarthritic knee does not relieve pain or improve function more than placebo surgery or optimized non-surgical care. Guideline panels have since recommended against arthroscopy for degenerative knee disease in most patients. This is why the policy treats the surgery as not medically necessary by default and does not cover pain, stiffness or swelling alone.

The covered pathway rests on a narrower and older clinical consensus: a fragment that physically blocks the joint (a loose body or a displaced tear) can be removed, and removing it can restore movement in a way exercise or medicine cannot. The evidence for this is mostly observational. A secondary analysis of a sham-controlled trial found that catching symptoms in knees with degenerative meniscal tears did not predict benefit from surgery, so the policy requires both a qualifying symptom and an imaging finding of a structure that can cause it, and asks for a trial of conservative care first unless the knee is truly locked.

Radiographic severity matters because results are worst in knees with advanced arthritis, where the main problem is lost cartilage across the joint rather than a removable fragment. The policy uses weight-bearing radiographs, which show joint-space narrowing more accurately than lying-down views. The time windows (3 months for symptoms, 6 months for MRI, 12 months for radiographs) are set so the evidence describes the knee as it is now; mechanical lesions can change quickly, while radiographic arthritis grade changes slowly.

Sources considered:

- Moseley JB et al., "A controlled trial of arthroscopic surgery for osteoarthritis of the knee," New England Journal of Medicine, 2002
- Kirkley A et al., "A randomized trial of arthroscopic surgery for osteoarthritis of the knee," New England Journal of Medicine, 2008
- Sihvonen R et al., "Arthroscopic partial meniscectomy versus sham surgery for a degenerative meniscal tear," New England Journal of Medicine, 2013
- Sihvonen R et al., "Mechanical symptoms and arthroscopic partial meniscectomy in patients with degenerative meniscus tear: a secondary analysis of a randomized trial," Annals of Internal Medicine, 2016
- Siemieniuk RAC et al., "Arthroscopic surgery for degenerative knee arthritis and meniscal tears: a clinical practice guideline," BMJ, 2017
- American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons, "Management of Osteoarthritis of the Knee (Non-Arthroplasty)," evidence-based clinical practice guideline, 3rd edition, 2021
- Bannuru RR et al., "OARSI guidelines for the non-surgical management of knee, hip, and polyarticular osteoarthritis," Osteoarthritis and Cartilage, 2019
- Kellgren JH, Lawrence JS, "Radiological assessment of osteo-arthrosis," Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases, 1957

| Section | Threshold | Confidence it is medically plausible | Note |

| 3.3 | Mechanical symptom documented within 3 months | High | Keeps the symptom current; a fragment can move or settle. |
| 3.4 | Weight-bearing radiographs within 12 months | High | Arthritis grade changes slowly; 12 months is a common look-back for joint imaging. |
| 3.5 / 7.5 | KL grade no more than 3 (grade 4 = severe) | Medium | Directionally right: outcomes are worst in end-stage knees. ### A.2 Expected ambiguities

| Section | Wording | Possible readings | Recommended reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1, 7.3 | "symptoms other than pain alone" | Any non-pain symptom (stiffness, swelling, giving way) qualifies; only a true mechanical block qualifies | Only a mechanical block. 7.3 makes this a closed list so the engine never has to judge it. |
| 7.3 | Note says "knee locks up" with no further description | Counts as locking; is pseudo-locking from pain | Insufficient evidence: the word alone doesn't meet 7.3, which needs a description. Request info for a symptom description. |
| 7.3 | "Gives way" or "buckles" | Mechanical symptom; quadriceps weakness or pain | Not a mechanical symptom unless the note also describes a block. Common in OA, so a good trap. |
| 7.3, 3.3 | Symptom reported by the member vs. seen by the clinician | History is enough; must be observed | History in a clinician note is enough for 3.3. Only 4.1(b) (locked knee) needs an exam finding. |
| 4.1(a) | "still documented after it ended" when care is ongoing | Needs an end date; ongoing care counts | Ongoing care counts if 12 weeks have passed and a note after that point documents the symptom. |
| 7.7(c) | 12 weeks vs. "3 months" | Calendar months; 84 days | 84 days, counted by code from start date to end date (7.10). Windows use calendar months per D-019; durations stay in days. |
| 7.6 | KL grade missing but report says "severe narrowing" | Treat as grade 4; treat as not shown | Not shown (insufficient evidence). Only "complete loss" or "bone-on-bone" maps to grade 4; "severe narrowing" can be KL 3. |
| 3.5 | Two reports in the window disagree on grade | Use the worst; use the most recent | Most recent, per 3.5, and flag the conflict, which sends the case to clinician review under the routing rules. |
| 4.2(a), 7.4 | MRI report hedges ("cannot exclude a displaced fragment") | Lesion shown; lesion not shown | Not shown, per 7.4. Mark insufficient evidence and flag for clinician review, since a surgeon's read may differ from the radiologist's wording. |
| 4.2(a) | MRI reports both a degenerative tear and a flap component | Degenerative tear excludes it; flap qualifies | The flap qualifies; 7.4 lists what does count, and a degenerative tear alongside it doesn't cancel it. |
| 2.2, 3.1 | Unspecified-side code (M17.10, M17.9) with side clear in notes | Outside policy; in scope with side from request | In scope; 3.1 checks the request names the side. Unspecified codes produce a request-info gap, not an out-of-scope escalation. |
| 3.2 | Bilateral diagnosis code (M17.0) on a one-knee request | Conflict with 3.2; consistent | Consistent: a bilateral code covers either knee. |
| 5.1.3 | Clinician says surgery is "high risk" but proceeds | Contraindication met; not met | Not met unless the note says surgery is unsafe now. "High risk" alone goes in the brief, not the contraindication. |

### A.3 Planned test complexity

Built for the kickoff's 20-case mix for this service:

- **Clean (6):** catching plus an MRI flap tear, KL 2, 14 weeks of PT and NSAID; a locked knee with a bucket-handle tear on MRI and no conservative care (pathway 4.1(b)); a loose body seen on radiograph only (pathway 4.2(b)); an unspecified-side diagnosis code with the side on the request.
- **Missing documentation (5):** no radiograph report; radiographs not stated as standing; MRI ordered but not resulted; PT records absent; "knee locks" with no description.
- **Clearly not met (3):** pain alone with no locking or catching (the demo case); bone-on-bone on radiographs; MRI showing only a degenerative tear.
- **Borderline (2):** conservative care of exactly 84 days vs. 83 days; MRI dated one day outside the 6-month window. KL grade exactly 3 is met.
- **Conflicting or outdated (2):** MRI of the left knee on a right-knee request; an older note describes catching and the most recent note says "pain only, no mechanical symptoms".
- **Buried contraindication (1):** a partial knee replacement of the requested knee mentioned only in the surgical history, or a prior scope 8 months ago.
- **Adversarial (1):** a PT note containing text telling the AI to approve.
