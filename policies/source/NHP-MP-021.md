# NHP-MP-021: Hypoglossal nerve stimulator implant for obstructive sleep apnea

**Northwind Health Plan · Medical Policy · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-01-01**

> Fictional policy for a non-commercial demo. Northwind Health Plan is not a real health plan. This document is not medical advice and does not describe any real payer's coverage.

## 1. Policy statement

A hypoglossal nerve stimulator is an implanted device that stimulates the nerve controlling the tongue during sleep, so the tongue moves forward and the airway stays open. Northwind covers the implant for adults with moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea who could not be treated with positive airway pressure, when the criteria in sections 3 and 4 are met and nothing in section 5 applies. Requests that do not clearly meet the criteria are reviewed by a Northwind clinician.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Service

| Northwind code | Service |
| --- | --- |
| NHP-P-1042 | Hypoglossal nerve stimulator implant |

This policy covers the first implant of a complete system (stimulator, stimulation lead and, where the device uses one, a breathing-sensing lead). Replacement, revision and removal are outside this policy (see 5.2.1).

| ICD-10-CM | Description |

A request whose primary diagnosis is not listed here is outside this policy and goes to clinician review.

### 2.3 Members

Northwind Medicare Advantage members, including members who qualify for Medicare through disability.

## 3. Coverage criteria

All of the following must be met unless a criterion says otherwise.

**3.1 Age.** The member's **age** is at least 22 years on the request date.

**3.2 Body mass index.** The member's **body mass index (BMI)** is less than 35.0 kg/m², using the **most recent value** documented within 6 months before the request date.

**3.3 Qualifying sleep study.** All of the following, using the **most recent value** from a **diagnostic sleep study**:

- (a) The study was performed within 24 months before the request date.
- (b) The **apnea-hypopnea index (AHI)** is at least 15 events per hour.
- (c) The AHI is no more than 65 events per hour.
- (d) The **central and mixed apnea share** is less than 25%.

**3.4 Airway endoscopy.** All of the following:

- (a) A **drug-induced sleep endoscopy (DISE)** was performed within 24 months before the request date.
- (b) The DISE report states that **complete concentric collapse** at the level of the soft palate was not seen.

**3.5 Sleep specialist evaluation.** All of the following:

- (a) The member had a face-to-face or video visit with a **sleep medicine clinician** within 12 months before the request date.
- (b) The note from that visit recommends hypoglossal nerve stimulation for this member.

## 4. Alternative pathways

All numbered items in this section are required.

**4.1 Positive airway pressure tried first.** Any one of the following:

- (a) **PAP failure**, as defined in 7.14
- (b) **PAP intolerance**, as defined in 7.15

## 5. Contraindications and exclusions

### 5.1 Contraindications

Coverage criteria are not met if any of the following is documented. These are medical reasons the implant is unsafe or unlikely to work for this member.

- **5.1.1** A neuromuscular disease that affects control of the tongue or upper airway, such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis or myasthenia gravis.
- **5.1.2** Weakness or paralysis of either hypoglossal nerve, from any cause.
- **5.1.3** Tonsil size of at least grade 3 on a 0 to 4 scale, on the most recent documented airway exam.
- **5.1.4** Another upper airway finding, such as a tongue-base mass or severe nasal obstruction, that a treating surgeon has documented needs correction before implant.
- **5.1.5** Chronic lung disease that requires supplemental oxygen while awake.
- **5.1.6** Pulmonary hypertension documented as moderate or severe.
- **5.1.7** Heart failure documented as New York Heart Association class III or class IV.
- **5.1.8** A heart attack (myocardial infarction) within 6 months before the request date.
- **5.1.9** Heart valve disease documented as severe.
- **5.1.10** Current pregnancy.
- **5.1.11** An expected, ongoing need for MRI scans that the planned device's labeling does not permit.
- **5.1.12** Another active implanted electrical device, such as a pacemaker, defibrillator or spinal cord stimulator, unless a treating clinician documents that interaction between the devices has been evaluated and is acceptable.
- **5.1.13** A cognitive, psychiatric or physical condition documented as preventing the member from operating the device's handheld remote, with no caregiver documented as able to do so.
- **5.1.14** Untreated narcolepsy or another untreated central disorder of excessive sleepiness.

### 5.2 Exclusions

Northwind does not cover the service under this policy in the following situations.

- **5.2.1** The member already has a hypoglossal nerve stimulator implanted. Replacement, revision and removal are reviewed outside this policy.
- **5.2.2** The request is for snoring alone, without obstructive sleep apnea that meets 3.3.
- **5.2.3** The planned device does not have US Food and Drug Administration marketing authorization for treating obstructive sleep apnea.

## 7. Definitions

**7.1 Request date.** The date Northwind receives a complete prior authorization request. All time windows in this policy count back from this date unless a criterion names another date.

**7.2 Documented.** Stated in a dated clinical record signed by a licensed clinician and included with the request. A statement made only on the request form is not documentation.

**7.3 Within N months before.** A window that starts on the same calendar day N months before the request date and ends on the request date, with both ends included. If that calendar day does not exist (for example, 31 April), the window starts on the last day of that month.

**7.4 Most recent value.** When the record holds more than one dated value for the same measure inside the criterion's window, the value with the latest date is used. If values from different sources disagree on the same latest date, or an earlier value in the window would change whether the criterion is met, the evidence is in conflict and the request goes to clinician review.

**7.5 Age.** The member's age in completed years on the request date, calculated from date of birth.

**7.6 Body mass index (BMI).** Weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters. A BMI recorded in the note is used as written. If no BMI is recorded, it is calculated from a height and a weight measured on the same date and rounded to one decimal place (0.05 rounds up). Weights in pounds and heights in inches are converted before calculating.

**7.7 Diagnostic sleep study.** An overnight study done to diagnose sleep apnea and interpreted by a physician: an attended in-lab polysomnogram, the diagnostic part of a split-night study, or a home sleep apnea test. A study done mainly to set PAP pressure (a titration study) is not a diagnostic sleep study.

**7.8 Apnea-hypopnea index (AHI).** The number of apneas plus hypopneas per hour of sleep, as stated in the diagnostic sleep study report. For a home sleep apnea test, the respiratory event index (events per hour of monitoring time) is used as the AHI. Hypopneas are counted using a 4% oxygen desaturation rule. When a report gives values for more than one rule, the 4% value is used. When a report does not name its rule, the reported value is used. When a report gives a value only for a different rule, the AHI criteria are not clearly met and the request goes to clinician review.

**7.9 Central and mixed apnea share.** The number of central apneas plus mixed apneas, divided by the total number of apneas and hypopneas, times 100, all from the same diagnostic sleep study. A report that gives these as events per hour may be used the same way (central plus mixed index divided by AHI).

**7.10 Drug-induced sleep endoscopy (DISE).** A procedure in which a clinician looks at the upper airway with a thin flexible camera while the member is sedated to imitate sleep, to see where and how the airway closes.

**7.11 Complete concentric collapse.** On DISE, a full closure of the airway at the soft palate in which the walls close in evenly from all sides, like a ring tightening. Partial concentric narrowing, or complete collapse in a front-to-back pattern, is not complete concentric collapse.

**7.12 Positive airway pressure (PAP) therapy.** Treatment with a bedside machine that blows pressurized air through a mask to hold the airway open, in any form: continuous (CPAP), auto-adjusting (APAP) or bilevel (BPAP).

**7.13 Adequate PAP use.** Device-recorded use of at least 4 hours per night on at least 70% of nights during a period of 30 consecutive days.

**7.14 PAP failure.** All of the following:

- (a) The member used PAP therapy.
- (b) While on PAP therapy, the AHI was at least 15 events per hour, shown by either an attended titration study at the best pressure the member tolerated, or a device usage report covering a 30-day period of adequate PAP use.

**7.15 PAP intolerance.** All of the following:

- (a) The member started PAP therapy.
- (b) A clinician documents that the member is unable to continue PAP therapy, or stopped using it.
- (c) The record names at least one specific reason, such as mask discomfort, claustrophobia, pressure intolerance, swallowing air, skin breakdown or sleep disrupted by the device.
- (d) The record shows at least one attempt to fix the problem before the member stopped, such as a mask change or refit, a pressure change, added humidification or a desensitization program.
- (e) A device usage report or a clinician note shows the member did not achieve adequate PAP use.

**7.16 Sleep medicine clinician.** A physician board-certified in sleep medicine, or a nurse practitioner or physician assistant working in a sleep medicine practice supervised by such a physician.

## 8. Clinical basis

Obstructive sleep apnea is repeated closing of the upper airway during sleep. Positive airway pressure works well for most patients who use it, but many cannot keep using it. Hypoglossal nerve stimulation is a second-line option for people who have tried PAP. The main randomized and cohort studies, and the registry data that followed, enrolled adults with moderate to severe sleep apnea, a limited body size, mostly obstructive (not central) events, and no circular collapse of the airway at the soft palate on sleep endoscopy. Results were worse in people outside those limits, which is why the criteria above mirror them.

- **Age at least 22** matches the adult population the devices were first studied and approved for.
- **BMI less than 35** sits between the early trial limit (around 32) and the wider limit now in device labeling (around 40). Response rates fall as BMI rises, so Northwind takes a middle position.
- **AHI from 15 to 65** keeps the service to moderate and severe disease. Device labeling now allows a higher upper limit; Northwind keeps the lower one because evidence at the top of the range is thinner.
- **Central and mixed apneas less than 25%** keeps the service to obstructive disease; stimulating the tongue does not help central apnea.
- **DISE** identifies the collapse pattern that predicts poor response.
- **PAP first** reflects guideline advice that PAP is first-line treatment and that patients who cannot use it should be referred for surgical options.
- **Adequate PAP use** (4 hours a night on 70% of nights across 30 days) is a widely used adherence benchmark.
- **Contraindications** reflect device labeling themes: conditions that stop the nerve or tongue from working, airway anatomy the device cannot fix, heart and lung disease that raises surgical risk or points to a different cause of symptoms, MRI and device interaction limits, and the need to operate the remote every night.

### A.1 Clinical plausibility flags

| Section | Threshold | Confidence it is medically plausible | Note |

| 3.1 | Age at least 22 | High | Matches the original adult approval. Labeling later added some adolescents with Down syndrome; irrelevant for an MA population but a reviewer may ask. |
| 3.2 | BMI less than 35.0 | High | Common payer cutoff. Labeling now goes to about 40, so Northwind is deliberately conservative. |
| 3.2 | BMI window of 6 months | Medium | Weight changes, so a recent value matters, but there is no standard window. 12 months would also be defensible. |
| 3.3(a) | Sleep study within 24 months | High | Typical across payers. Sleep apnea severity can change with weight, so older studies are unreliable. |
| 3.3(b) | AHI at least 15 | High | Standard threshold for moderate disease. |
| 3.3(c) | AHI no more than 65 | Medium | Plausible and widely used, but labeling now allows up to about 100. Members with AHI between 65 and 100 will go to clinician review; confirm that is the behavior you want. |
| 3.3(d) | Central and mixed less than 25% | High | Standard. |
| 3.4(a) | DISE within 24 months | Low | Many policies require DISE but set no window. I added one to create date-boundary test cases; a medical director might call it arbitrary. |
| 3.5(a) | Sleep visit within 12 months | Medium | Reasonable pre-surgical recency, but not a known standard. |
| 5.1.3 | Tonsils at least grade 3 | Medium | Large tonsils are a recognized exclusion for this implant; the exact grade cutoff varies. |
| 5.1.5 | Daytime oxygen as the marker of severe lung disease | Medium | My own testable stand-in for "severe lung disease". Clinically sensible, but other plans use lung-function numbers. |

### A.2 Expected ambiguities

| Section | Wording | Possible readings | Recommended reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.2, 7.6 | BMI at the cutoff | A recorded 34.96 could be read as 35.0 if rounded; recorded and calculated BMI can differ | Use a recorded BMI as written, with no rounding; calculate only when none is recorded. Written into 7.6. |
| 3.3, 7.4 | Which sleep study counts | Most recent study; any qualifying study in the window | Most recent diagnostic study, and flag a conflict if an earlier in-window study would change the result. Written into 7.4. |
| 7.8 | Hypopnea scoring rule | 4% rule only; any rule the lab used | 4% when given; accept the reported value when no rule is named; clinician review when only a 3% or arousal-based value is given. Written into 7.8. Needs your sign-off (see decision log). |
| 7.8 | Home test REI as AHI | REI counts; only in-lab AHI counts | REI counts. Home tests are standard for diagnosis and tend to underestimate, which errs toward not qualifying. |
| 7.9 | Denominator for central share | All apneas and hypopneas; apneas only | All apneas and hypopneas, which matches "share of the AHI". Apneas-only gives a higher percentage and would exclude more members. |
| 3.4(b) | DISE report silent on soft-palate pattern | Silence means no collapse; silence is missing evidence | Missing evidence: insufficient, so request info. The criterion asks the report to state it. |
| 3.4(b), 7.11 | "Near-complete" or "predominantly concentric" collapse | Counts as complete concentric; does not | Not clearly met, so clinician review. Expect the extractor to quote the phrase and the router to send it to a person. |

| 4.1, 7.14 | Failure on symptoms alone | Persistent sleepiness despite adequate use is failure; only a residual AHI counts | Only the residual AHI counts for approve-ready. Symptom-based failure goes to clinician review, where a person can weigh it. |
| 4.1 | Age of the PAP trial | Any past trial counts; trial must be recent | Any past trial counts. No window is set on purpose; flagged so you can add one. |
| 5.1.11 | MRI need versus device labeling | Any planned MRI; only MRI the device cannot accept | Only MRI the device cannot accept. Needs device-specific facts, so expect clinician review when an MRI need is documented. |
| 5.1.12 | "Unless ... evaluated and acceptable" | Any note mentioning the device is enough; a specific statement is needed | A specific clinician statement about device interaction. A bare mention of a pacemaker with no such statement is a contraindication found, so clinician review. |

### A.3 Planned test complexity

Built for the kickoff case mix (20 HGNS cases):

- **Borderline numeric (about 2 cases):** BMI recorded as 35.0 (not met) versus 34.9 (met); BMI only in pounds and inches that calculates to 34.96 (rounds to 35.0, not met); AHI exactly 15.0 or exactly 65.0 (met) versus 65.4 (not met); central share exactly 25% (not met); age 21 on the request date with a 22nd birthday the following week.
- **Time windows:** sleep study exactly 24 months before the request date (inside, per 7.3) versus 24 months and 1 day (outside); DISE 25 months old; BMI from 7 months ago with nothing newer.
- **Conflicting or outdated evidence (about 2 cases):** two BMIs in the window on different dates; an older qualifying sleep study next to a newer one with AHI 13; a copy-forward note repeating last year's BMI.
- **Missing documentation (about 5 cases):** no DISE report; PAP intolerance claimed with no troubleshooting step (7.15(d)); sleep report without central apnea counts; no device usage report or note on hours of use.
- **Pathway logic:** one case meets 4.1 only through failure, one only through intolerance, one through neither.
- **Buried contraindication (1 case):** pacemaker appears only in the medication or device list on page 30; or myasthenia gravis only in the problem list.
- **Scoring-rule edge:** a home test reporting only a 3% AHI (clinician review per 7.8).
- **Adversarial (1 case):** a note containing text telling the reviewer or system to approve.
