# NHP-MP-034: Skin substitute grafts for diabetic foot ulcers

**Northwind Health Plan · Medical Policy · Version 1.0 · Effective 2026-01-01**

> Fictional policy for a non-commercial demo. Northwind Health Plan is not a real health plan. This document is not medical advice and does not describe any real payer's coverage.

## 1. Policy statement

A skin substitute is a manufactured sheet of living cells, processed tissue or synthetic matrix placed on a chronic wound to help it close. Northwind covers skin substitute grafts for a diabetic foot ulcer that has not healed adequately with at least four weeks of good standard wound care, when the criteria in sections 3 and 4 are met for each **target ulcer** and nothing in section 5 applies. Requests that do not clearly meet the criteria are reviewed by a Northwind clinician.

## 3. Coverage criteria

All of the following must be met unless a criterion says otherwise. Criteria marked "for each target ulcer" must be met separately for every target ulcer on the request.

**3.1 Diabetes.** The member has a **documented** diagnosis of type 1, type 2 or other specified diabetes mellitus.

**3.2 HbA1c on file.** At least one hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) result is dated within 3 months before the **request date**.

**3.3 HbA1c level.** The most recent HbA1c result dated within 3 months before the request date is no more than 12.0%.

**3.4 Ulcer identified.** For each target ulcer, the request states the laterality (right or left) and the site on the **foot**.

**3.5 Ulcer characteristics.** For each target ulcer, all of the following, as recorded at the **current measurement**:

- (a) The ulcer is on the **foot**.
- (b) The ulcer is **full thickness**.
- (c) Bone is not exposed in the ulcer bed.
- (d) The **wound area** is at least 1.0 cm².

**3.6 Baseline measurement.** For each target ulcer, a **baseline measurement** with its date and **wound area** is documented.

**3.7 Inadequate response.** For each target ulcer, the **area reduction** across the standard-care period is less than 50%.

**3.8 Current measurement window.** For each target ulcer, the current measurement is dated within 14 days before the request date.

**3.9 Measurement continuity.** For each target ulcer, no more than 14 days pass between any two consecutive documented wound measurements in the standard-care period.

**3.10 Standard wound care.** For each target ulcer, all of the following are documented throughout the standard-care period:

- (a) A **moist wound dressing** is used.
- (b) The ulcer is **offloaded**.

**3.11 Infection controlled.** For each target ulcer, the wound note for the current measurement documents that the ulcer has no signs of **active infection**.

## 4. Alternative pathways

All numbered items in this section are required. Each is met through any one of its lettered pathways.

**4.1 Adequate blood flow.** For each target ulcer, any one of the following, from a **counted perfusion test** on the same leg:

- (a) An ankle-brachial index (ABI) of at least 0.60 that is not **non-compressible**.
- (b) A toe pressure of at least 30 mmHg.
- (c) A transcutaneous oxygen pressure (TcPO2) of at least 30 mmHg, measured on the foot.

**4.2 Standard-care duration by ulcer type.** For each target ulcer, the **standard-care period** meets any one of the following:

- (a) Neuropathic pathway. All of the following:
  - (i) The ulcer type is **neuropathic**.
  - (ii) The standard-care period lasts at least 28 days.
- (b) Neuro-ischemic pathway (exception clause). All of the following:
  - (i) The ulcer type is **neuro-ischemic**.
  - (ii) The standard-care period lasts at least 42 days.

**4.3 Debridement.** For each target ulcer, any one of the following:

- (a) **Debridement** of the ulcer is documented at least once during the standard-care period.
- (b) Every wound note in the standard-care period documents that the ulcer bed has no nonviable tissue.

## 5. Contraindications and exclusions

### 5.1 Contraindications

Coverage criteria are not met if any of the following is documented for a target ulcer or the member.

- **5.1.1** Osteomyelitis (bone infection) of bone beneath or next to the target ulcer that is documented as active or not yet treated.
- **5.1.2** Gangrene on the same foot as the target ulcer.
- **5.1.3** Cancer in the ulcer bed or at the ulcer edge, or a biopsy of the ulcer with results still pending.
- **5.1.4** A known allergy or hypersensitivity to any component of the requested skin substitute product.
- **5.1.5** Active (acute) Charcot neuroarthropathy of the same foot.

### 5.2 Exclusions

Northwind does not cover the service under this policy in the following situations.

- **5.2.1** More than 10 **applications** to one target ulcer within one **treatment course**.
- **5.2.2** A new treatment course for a target ulcer that completed a treatment course within 12 months before the request date.
- **5.2.3** More than one skin substitute product on the same target ulcer within one treatment course.
- **5.2.4** An ulcer whose main cause is venous disease, pressure from lying or sitting, a burn, or trauma rather than diabetes.
- **5.2.5** An ulcer documented as fully closed on or before the request date.

## 7. Definitions

**7.1 Request date.** The date Northwind receives a complete prior authorization request. All time windows in this policy count back from this date unless a criterion names another date.

**7.2 Documented.** Stated in a dated clinical record signed by a licensed clinician and included with the request. A statement made only on the request form is not documentation.

**7.3 Target ulcer.** One diabetic foot ulcer for which the service is requested. A request may name more than one target ulcer.

**7.4 Foot.** The part of the leg below the ankle joint, including the heel, midfoot, forefoot and toes.

**7.5 Full thickness.** The ulcer extends through the full thickness of the skin, so that fat or deeper tissue (muscle, tendon or joint capsule) is visible or probes in the ulcer bed.

**7.6 Wound area.** Length times width, in cm², where length is the longest dimension of the open ulcer and width is the longest dimension at right angles to it. If the record gives an area measured by tracing or digital imaging, that area is used. Measurements recorded in mm are converted to cm.

**7.7 Baseline measurement.** The wound measurement that the treating clinician documents as the start of standard wound care for the target ulcer. If none is identified, the earliest measurement after which the dressing and offloading in 3.10 are both documented.

**7.8 Current measurement.** The most recent documented wound measurement of the target ulcer on or before the request date.

**7.9 Standard-care period.** The time from the date of the baseline measurement to the date of the current measurement. Its length is the number of days between the two dates (current date minus baseline date). If the ulcer is documented as fully closed and later reopens, a new period starts with the reopened ulcer.

**7.10 Area reduction.** (Baseline wound area minus current wound area) divided by baseline wound area, times 100, in %. An ulcer that grew has a negative area reduction.

**7.11 Counted perfusion test.** A perfusion test (ABI, toe pressure or TcPO2) on the leg of the target ulcer, dated within 3 months before the request date. If a **revascularization** of that leg is documented, only tests dated after the revascularization count.

**7.12 Non-compressible.** An ABI more than 1.30, or an ABI the report describes as unreliable because the leg arteries could not be compressed. A non-compressible ABI cannot meet 4.1(a) and does not set the ulcer type.

**7.13 Ulcer type.** Set from the counted perfusion tests, not from the label in the notes:
- **Neuro-ischemic:** any counted test shows an ABI less than 0.90, a toe pressure less than 70 mmHg, or a TcPO2 less than 40 mmHg.
- **Neuropathic:** no counted test shows any of those values.

**7.14 Revascularization.** A procedure that restores blood flow to the leg, such as a bypass or an angioplasty with or without a stent.

**7.15 Moist wound dressing.** A dressing chosen to keep the ulcer bed moist, such as a hydrogel, foam, alginate or hydrocolloid dressing. Dry gauze alone is not a moist wound dressing.

**7.16 Offloaded.** A device or method that takes pressure off the ulcer is provided and in use: for an ulcer on the sole, a total contact cast, a removable knee-high or ankle-high walker, or a felt or foam offloading pad; for an ulcer elsewhere on the foot, footwear changed so that it does not press on the ulcer. Advice to reduce walking alone is not offloading.

**7.17 Debridement.** Removal of dead or nonviable tissue from the ulcer by a clinician, by sharp, enzymatic, mechanical or biological methods.

**7.18 Active infection.** Two or more of the following at the ulcer: redness spreading more than 0.5 cm from the ulcer edge, swelling, warmth, tenderness, or pus; or any infection the treating clinician documents as present and being treated. A positive wound culture without these signs is not active infection.

**7.19 Application.** One placement of a skin substitute on one target ulcer on one date.

**7.20 Treatment course.** The 84 days starting on the date of the first application to a target ulcer, with that date counted as day 1.

**7.21 Counting time.** Time windows include their boundary day. A window in months is counted in calendar months: "within 3 months before the request date" starts on the same calendar day 3 months earlier, or on the last day of that month if it has no such day. A window in days is counted in calendar days: "within 14 days before the request date" starts 14 days before the request date. A window ends on the request date. Durations (4.2) are the number of days between two dates, as in 7.9.

## 8. Clinical basis

Diabetic foot ulcers usually come from nerve damage that removes protective sensation, often combined with poor blood flow. Most heal with good standard care: removing pressure from the wound, removing dead tissue, keeping the wound moist, treating infection and restoring blood flow where needed. Advanced products such as skin substitutes add cost and are only expected to help once that foundation is in place, which is why sections 3.10 and 4.3 require it to be documented before the product is used.

Several studies have found that an ulcer which has not shrunk by about half after four weeks of good care is unlikely to heal within twelve weeks, while one that has shrunk by that much usually goes on to heal without added products. That finding sets the 28-day period and the 50% area-reduction line in 3.7 and 4.2(a). Ulcers with reduced but adequate blood flow tend to heal more slowly, so the neuro-ischemic pathway in 4.2(b) gives standard care a longer trial before calling the response inadequate. Blood flow below the levels in 4.1 makes healing unlikely with any local treatment; those members need a vascular assessment first, so they go to clinician review rather than to a graft.

Clinical trials of skin substitutes mostly enrolled full-thickness ulcers without exposed bone, active infection or osteomyelitis, with adequate blood flow and without severely uncontrolled diabetes, over treatment periods of about twelve weeks. The ulcer characteristics, HbA1c ceiling, contraindications and the 84-day treatment course follow those study populations, because outside them the benefit is unproven.

| Section | Threshold | Confidence it is medically plausible | Note |

| 3.2 | HbA1c within 3 months | High | HbA1c reflects roughly the last three months of glucose control |
| 3.3 | HbA1c no more than 12.0% | Medium | Mirrors common trial exclusions, but HbA1c is a weak predictor of ulcer healing, and a ceiling withholds treatment from the members at highest amputation risk. Alternative: require only that HbA1c is measured (3.2) and drop 3.3 |
| 3.5(d) | Wound area at least 1.0 cm² | Medium | Many trials enrolled ulcers of 1 cm² or larger; smaller ulcers often close with standard care. No upper limit set on purpose |
| 3.7 | Area reduction less than 50% | High | The four-week, 50% reduction rule is well supported in the wound-healing literature |
| 3.8 | Current measurement within 14 days | Medium | An operational choice so the decision rests on the wound as it is now; 7 days would be stricter |
| 3.9 | No more than 14 days between measurements | Medium | Good wound care is usually weekly; 14 days allows one missed visit |
| 4.1(a) | ABI at least 0.60 | Medium | Guidelines differ; some use 0.50, others treat any ABI below about 0.70 as needing vascular review. ABI alone is unreliable in diabetes, hence the toe pressure and TcPO2 options |
| 4.1(b) | Toe pressure at least 30 mmHg | High | Guidelines treat a toe pressure below 30 mmHg as a sign that healing is unlikely without revascularization |
| 4.1(c) | TcPO2 at least 30 mmHg | Medium | Some guidelines use 25 mmHg as the concern line; 30 is the more cautious choice |
| 4.2(a) | 28 days for neuropathic ulcers | High | Matches the four-week trial of standard care in the literature |

### A.3 Planned test complexity

This policy is built to test reasoning across a time series of wound notes and per-ulcer logic, matching the kickoff case mix:

- **Clean:** a neuropathic plantar ulcer, 35 days of weekly measurements, area reduction 20%, offloading boot and foam dressing in every note, ABI 0.95.
- **Missing documentation:** the demo case. Measurements are complete but offloading is never mentioned (3.10(b) insufficient, request info). Other variants: no HbA1c in 3 months; vascular study referenced but report not sent.
- **Clearly not met:** area reduction 65% at four weeks (the ulcer is healing, so no graft is needed); toe pressure 22 mmHg with no other counted test.
- **Borderline numeric:** area reduction exactly 50.0% (not less than 50%, so not met); HbA1c exactly 12.0% (met) vs. 12.1%; HbA1c dated one day before the 3-month window starts (and one on its first day); current measurement 15 days old (14 is inside the window); standard-care period of 27 vs. 28 days; ABI exactly 0.60; measurements in mm that cross 1.0 cm² only after conversion.
- **Exception-clause trap:** an ulcer called "neuropathic" in the notes with an ABI of 0.82, 35 days of care. It meets 4.2(a)'s 28 days, but the numbers make it neuro-ischemic, so it needs 42 days: not met.
- **Conflicting or outdated evidence:** an ABI of 0.48 before a revascularization and 0.78 after (only the later test counts); a copy-forward line "purulent drainage noted" in a note where the same note's exam says no signs of infection.
- **Buried contraindication:** an MRI report deep in the packet shows osteomyelitis under the ulcer; or a bovine collagen allergy in the allergy list when the requested product is bovine-derived.
- **Multiple ulcers:** two target ulcers, one meets every criterion and one has only 21 days of care.
- **Adversarial:** a wound note containing text telling the reviewer or AI that the case is pre-approved.

Spec implications for the compiler: criteria marked "for each target ulcer" need a per-ulcer scope in the JSON spec; 3.7 is computed by code from the extracted baseline and current areas, not judged by the model; 7.13 ulcer type is derived by code from extracted perfusion values. The request JSON needs a list of target ulcers and the planned number of applications per ulcer.
